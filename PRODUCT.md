# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Players of the Pokémon Light Platinum DS ROM hack, mid-game, usually with the DS
in one hand and a phone in the other. They arrive with one specific question — what
lives on Route 404, which badge unlocks Fly, where the Sun Palace levers go — and
want the answer without reading around it. The phone is propped beside a lit
console, often in a dim room, and gets a glance at a time.

## Product Purpose

A field guide to a game that quietly stops behaving like the HeartGold it is built
on. It exists because the hack's own text, tables and encounter lists are the only
trustworthy source and nobody else has read them out. Success is a player finding
the fact they came for in one scan, trusting it because it is sourced, and leaving.

## Brand Personality

Wayfinding. The guide behaves like the signage of a transit network for the Zhery
region: it tells you where you are, what is here, and how to get to the next thing.
Three words: clear, direct, sourced. The voice states what the cartridge says and
says plainly when it does not know. Colour and number are instruments, never
ornament — a colour names a line, a number is a route, a level or a dex entry.

## Anti-references

- Fan-wiki sprawl: walls of unsourced prose, rumour presented as fact.
- Marketing-page rhythm on reference surfaces: hero sections, eyebrows, progress
  theatre ("7 of 15 visited") where no progress is being tracked.
- Reading layouts for lookup tasks: a 57-item index squeezed into a prose column.
- Decorative states: chips, thumbnails and "done" markers that carry no meaning.
- Signage cosplay: fake station codes, invented sequence numbers, arrows that point
  nowhere. The system borrows how signs work, not how they look in photographs.
- Grey-on-grey neutrality: a page where nothing says which section you are in.

## Design Principles

1. **The row is the answer.** A place's row should contain what the player came
   for; no click to reveal what could have been shown.
2. **Say only what the name doesn't.** "Yellow Town · Town" is one word too many.
3. **Density is respect.** Dense, aligned, scannable beats airy and pretty on a
   lookup page. Save air for the chapters.
4. **Empty is a sentence, not a row.** Undocumented places are listed in one quiet
   line, never as a run of blank rows.
5. **Sourced or silent.** If the game doesn't state it, the guide doesn't either.
6. **Colour names the line.** The four line colours say which part of the guide you
   are in and nothing else. Yellow says "you are here". Type plates keep their hues.
7. **Numbers are real or absent.** A shield holds a route number, a gym's order, a
   dex number or a level. Nothing is numbered for rhythm.
8. **A list is shaped by what is in it.** Rows for what you read across, stops on a
   line for what comes in order, a ruled sheet for what you know by its picture.
   Never the same card repeated.
9. **Both languages, the cartridge's words.** The whole guide reads in English or
   Spanish. Names come from the games — the official Spanish for types, moves and
   items, the ROM's own Spanish for the cities it is known to name. A name the
   guide cannot source stays as it was recorded rather than being invented.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Every page is built in English and in Spanish (`/es/`), with `lang` and
`hreflang` set, and the language switch is in the bar at every width.

Body text is never lighter than `--ink-3` (5.1:1 or better on every surface, both
themes). Signal yellow (`--accent`) never carries text; it sits behind `--on-accent`
ink at 11.7:1. White on any line colour clears 4.8:1. Links are underlined, not
colour-only. Focus is a yellow ring with an ink inner ring, visible on light, dark
and sign surfaces. Reduced motion honoured on every transition. Touch targets ≥44px
on interactive rows; the site is used on phones.
